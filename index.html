Recursive structure

Recursion runs only through System One: every operational element contains a whole viable system, and its metasystemic functions follow it down. Open any operation to step into it, or zoom into it in the model. The highlighted node is the System-in-Focus.

    What the model describes

    Stafford Beer developed the Viable System Model in Brain of the Firm (1972), The Heart of Enterprise (1979) and Diagnosing the System for Organizations (1985). It sets out the functions any system needs to remain viable: to keep a separate existence in an environment that keeps changing.

    The operational elements of System One produce the system. The metasystem (Systems Two to Five, with Three-Star) exists to serve them: System Two damps oscillation, System Three manages the inside-and-now, System Four the outside-and-then, and System Five supplies closure and identity. Each operational element is itself a viable system, so the same structure repeats at every level of recursion, and only through System One.

    Terms follow Beer's books as collected in Pure VSM (cybernetic-futures wiki).

    Channels in this model

    Beer lists six vertical channels in Diagnosing the System: Corporate Intervention, the Resource Bargain (with accountability), operational linkages, environmental intersects, System Two, and audit. The central command axis is drawn as two lines, Corporate Intervention and the Resource Bargain, running from System Three to each local management, which is the operation's own metasystem. The Three-Star audit is the sporadic exception that reaches the operations directly, from the environment side. The algedonic signal rises from every local management parallel to the Resource Bargain, behind Systems Three, Four and Five, to System Five; in 3D it runs on a plane behind the model.

    System Five links to System Four and monitors the Three–Four homeostat; it has no direct line to System Three.

    Two extensions can be switched on or off in the Model panel: System Four feeding the Resource Bargain, which reads the command axis as anticipatory, and a direct link between System One and System Four. Neither appears in the commonly reproduced diagram.

    The drawing convention switches between a square metasystem box holding 3, 3*, 4, 5 and the System Two centre, with local managements tilted 45 degrees, and a rhombus metasystem with upright local managements. The System Two centre sits inside the metasystem; the Bridge option draws it on the boundary and, in 3D, puts all of System Two on its own plane between operations and metasystem. It is an interpretive option, prompted by a Stanford Medicine study (Nature Neuroscience, September 2026) reporting that the forebrain and midbrain arise from one progenitor lineage and the hindbrain from another.

    Data flows and recursion

    The Flows tab names what travels along each channel and in which direction; the moving dots follow those directions. The coverage bar shows how many channels carry something. An empty channel is worth asking about: in a real organization it often marks a missing function.

    Load the model company to see a bicycle maker mapped across three levels. Fill gaps with standard flows gives any level a neutral starting point. In a Claude artifact you can also describe an organization and let Claude map it, then map each operation one level down from the inspector.

    When you open an operation, its environment becomes the environment one level down and its local management becomes the metasystem, following Beer's recursion. Scroll toward any operation to zoom into it: the next recursion sits inside each circle, turned 45 degrees, and becomes the System-in-Focus as you arrive. Zoom far out to rise a level.

    How the 3D view uses depth

    The 3D model keeps the canonical layout and adds one dimension: the environment sits at the back, the operations in the middle, the metasystem in front. Channels that cross between domains become visible as slopes. Each solid is chosen so that seen face-on it is exactly Beer's symbol: spheres for circles, cubes for squares, prisms for triangles, an octahedron for the rhombus. The Depth slider controls the separation.
