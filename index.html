Recursive structure

Every System 1 operation contains a whole viable system. Open any branch to step into it; the highlighted node is the level in focus.

    What the model describes

    Stafford Beer developed the Viable System Model in Brain of the Firm (1972), The Heart of Enterprise (1979) and Diagnosing the System for Organizations (1985). It sets out the functions any system needs to remain viable: to keep a separate existence in an environment that keeps changing.

    The operations (System 1) do the work. The metasystem (Systems 2 to 5) exists to serve them: it coordinates, manages the whole in the present, looks to the future, and holds identity. Each operation is itself a viable system, so the same structure repeats at every level of recursion.

    Beer's symbols

    AmoebaEnvironment: fuzzy, changing, of far greater variety than the system
    CircleOperation: a System 1 unit that does the primary work
    SquareManagement: local management of an operation, and Systems 3, 4, 5
    TriangleSystem 2 coordination (pointing up) and System 3* audit (pointing down)
    RhombusMetasystem: the management of the whole, enclosing 3, 4 and 5
    Algedonic channelAlarm signals from operations straight to System 5

    How the 3D view uses depth

    The 3D model keeps the canonical layout and adds one dimension: the environment sits at the back, the operations in the middle, the metasystem in front. Channels that cross between domains become visible as slopes. Each solid is chosen so that seen face-on it is exactly Beer's symbol: spheres for circles, cubes for squares, prisms for triangles, an octahedron for the rhombus. The Depth slider controls the separation.
